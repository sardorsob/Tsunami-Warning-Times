# PacificVis 2027 and tsunami-event data feasibility

- **Status:** ready for a data-proof spike, not ready to freeze the event
- **Sources accessed:** 2026-09-04
- **Scope:** official contest requirements and a primary-source feasibility comparison; no datasets were downloaded
- **Workflow:** Standard/direct Core route; primary catastrophe lane = event forecast/intelligence; selected domain skill = `geo-data-engineering` because the decision turns on source contracts, formats, CRS/time metadata, and reproducible acquisition. No secondary lane.

## Decision summary

Use the **11 March 2011 Great Tohoku tsunami** as the provisional event. It has the best evidenced combination of a mature NOAA event-specific MOST analysis, machine-queryable NCEI travel-time contours, a 35-station event DART package, modeled-versus-observed tide-gauge comparisons spanning the Pacific, and reviewed USGS source metadata. The recommendation remains conditional on one short data-proof spike: prove that the NCEI contour geometry and raw model/station records can support the intended animation and reproducible arrival picks without digitizing figures.

Do **not** describe the principal cross-community quantity as “public warning time.” The basin-wide quantity currently supportable for all selected places is **physical travel time / rupture-to-observed-arrival**. An official-message lead time can be added only where an archived bulletin is tied to a named issuing center and community; a public-alert lead time needs a separate, timestamped local dissemination record.

## Project choices (not contest rules)

The following are internal scope choices from the handoff, not obligations imposed by PacificVis: a coded React/TypeScript/D3 browser story; one historical Pacific event; one authoritative travel-time product; a small DART/tide-gauge chain; four to six communities; a reproducible Python preprocessing pipeline; and a guided story before optional exploration. NOAA preference, a Pacific-centered projection, Vercel-style deployment, and any Bayesian analysis are also project choices.

## What the candidate model products actually are

Two NOAA products must not be conflated:

- **NCEI Tsunami Travel Time (TTT):** a first-arrival calculation from an earthquake epicenter, using water depth as the governing variable. NCEI says its archived maps use TTT software and 2-arc-minute ETOPO2 bathymetry; they contain arrival time, not wave height, and document several reasons modeled and observed arrivals may differ. ([method and caveats](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/travel-time-maps)) NCEI exposes the 2010 Maule and 2011 Tohoku hourly isochrones as EPSG:4326 polyline feature layers supporting JSON, GeoJSON, and PBF. ([layer index](https://gis.ngdc.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/ttt_contours/MapServer), [Tohoku layer schema](https://gis.ngdc.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/ttt_contours/MapServer/17)) These are machine-readable contours, not a verified continuous arrival-time raster.
- **NCTR MOST forecast/research output:** an event source assembled from precomputed unit-source simulations and constrained by DART observations. NCTR says its propagation database is composed of precomputed unit-source model runs and can produce first- and maximum-wave arrival quantities. ([database description](https://nctr.pmel.noaa.gov/propagation-database.html)) The event pages label their results **research products, not official forecasts**, and must not be represented as the warning centers' issued forecast. NCTR documents netCDF access through an OPeNDAP/THREDDS catalog, but this audit did not verify a working event-specific raw grid for any candidate. ([access documentation](https://nctr.pmel.noaa.gov/propagation-database-access.html))

### Licensing/terms finding shared by all three

NCEI policy says NOAA- or other Federal-agency-produced environmental data are openly available and in the U.S. public domain unless explicitly exempt, while non-Federal archived content can retain provider rights. ([NCEI Open Data Policy](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/NCEI%20PD-10-2-02%20-%20Open%20Data%20Policy%20Signed.pdf)) NCEI gives durable citation guidance and DOI links for [DART](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/dart-ocean-bottom-pressure) and [tide-gauge](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/tide-gauge-data) collections. USGS likewise states that USGS-authored information is in the U.S. public domain but third-party items may not be. ([USGS copyright and credit](https://www.usgs.gov/information-policies-and-instructions/copyrights-and-credits))

That establishes good **policy-level** reuse discoverability, not a per-file license audit. The event pages do not expose a machine-readable license beside every model graphic, ArcGIS feature, or station file. Capture each final asset's own metadata/credit statement before publication; credit NOAA/PMEL/NCTR and USGS even when permission is not required, and do not reuse third-party basemap or partner imagery merely because it appears on a Federal page.

## Honest “warning time” contract

All timestamps must be normalized to UTC internally while preserving source precision and the original time field.

| Label | Calculation | What it supports | What it does **not** support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rupture-to-arrival** or **physical travel time** | observed arrival timestamp − catalog earthquake origin timestamp | Comparable basin-crossing duration. NCEI defines travel time as the time to the first arriving wave at a runup location, but warns that historical sources may actually report a later wave. ([NCEI definitions/caveat](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/global-historical-data)) | Not warning issuance, alert receipt, decision time, or evacuation time. The catalog origin is a reference instant for a rupture that unfolds over time. |
| **Modeled first-arrival time** | model ETA at location, with model/version/source/bathymetry named | Prediction-versus-observation comparison. TTT is a bathymetry-based first-arrival estimate; an NCTR forecast is a different, source-constrained model product. ([NCEI TTT](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/natural-hazards/tsunamis-earthquakes-volcanoes/tsunamis/travel-time-maps), [NCTR forecast overview](https://nctr.pmel.noaa.gov/tsunami-forecast.html)) | Not an observed arrival and not necessarily an issued operational forecast. |
| **Forecast/bulletin issuance-to-arrival lead** | observed arrival − timestamp of a specific center's specific message revision | Time remaining after the issuing center published that bulletin. Tsunami.gov event records distinguish origin time, message issue time, forecast arrival, and observation time. ([Tsunami.gov help](https://www.tsunami.gov/?page=help)) | Not proof that residents received, understood, or acted on the message. Use the issuing center, product type, revision number, and affected area in the label. |
| **Public-alert lead time** | observed arrival − timestamp of a named public dissemination event for that locality/channel | A communication-system measure, but only when an authoritative siren, WEA, EAS, local alert, or equivalent record exists. NOAA's warning guide distinguishes center dissemination from notification/understanding. ([NOAA warning-center guide](https://nctr.pmel.noaa.gov/education/IOTWS/CONOPS/TWC_All_lowres.pdf)) FEMA's IPAWS archive contains CAP messages from 2012 onward and can support a 2025 U.S. alert-sent analysis. ([FEMA IPAWS archive](https://gis.fema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/FEMA/IPAWS_Archive/FeatureServer)) | A CAP `sent` timestamp is not individual receipt, comprehension, decision, or evacuation completion. It cannot provide a comparable U.S. series for the 2010/2011 candidates because that archive begins in 2012. |
| **Observed first deviation** | first sample at which de-tided residual exceeds a predeclared detection rule for a sustained duration | A reproducible instrument pick if threshold, filter, cadence, missingness rule, and uncertainty are recorded. NCEI event files provide raw observation, fitted tide, and residual columns. ([Tohoku DART schema](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/hazard/dart/2011honshu_dart.html)) | Not automatically identical to a catalog “arrival,” a first crest, or an operational detector trigger. It is method- and cadence-dependent. |
| **Maximum-wave time** | timestamp of the predeclared maximum quantity within a documented event window—for example, highest positive de-tided residual | Shows why the first wave need not be the largest. The IOC/ITIC glossary separately treats initial arrival and maximum-wave arrival as forecastable properties. ([IOC/ITIC glossary](https://legacy.itic.ioc-unesco.org/legacy.itic.ioc-unesco.org/index77b8.html?Itemid=2306&id=2078&option=com_content&view=article)) | Not first arrival. Do not switch silently among highest crest, maximum absolute residual, and peak-to-trough wave height. |

Recommended public wording for the first complete version: **“time from earthquake origin to observed first arrival”** for the comparable community panels, and **“official message issued X before observed arrival”** only for substories with a verified bulletin. Reserve “usable warning time” for a later, separately sourced analysis that includes local dissemination and response milestones.

## Event recommendation and why it is conditional

Select **2011 Tohoku** for the present thesis, because it uniquely combines:

1. a machine-queryable official TTT isochrone layer suitable for a browser map;
2. mature event-specific NCTR modeling with explicit source coefficients and broad modeled/observed comparisons;
3. a large, event-packaged DART record and separately archived NOAA tide records;
4. a credible six-place candidate pool across distinct Pacific geometries; and
5. reviewed USGS origin metadata.

This is a **data-backed provisional selection**, not permission to claim that the intended equal-distance comparison already exists. The deciding scientific discovery must survive the next spike. If the product is reframed around actual operational-message timing rather than physical propagation, reassess the choice: the 2025 Kamchatka archive has much better verified bulletin provenance, but currently weaker/more preliminary model-field evidence.

## Required next data-proof steps

1. **Prove the TTT geometry.** Query NCEI layer 17 as GeoJSON; record request URL, access date, feature count, `HOURS` values, CRS, antimeridian behavior, geometry validity, and missing contours. Compare three coordinates/times manually with the official rendered map. Do not interpolate a continuous raster from contours until its error is measured.
2. **Resolve the animation field.** Test the documented NCTR THREDDS/OPeNDAP catalog and locate the Tohoku unit-source files named on the event page. Confirm variables, units, time step, grid, longitude convention, and the event's linear-combination coefficients. If raw access or reuse terms remain unclear, contact NCTR; otherwise scope the story to authoritative isochrones rather than inventing a “smooth modeled field.”
3. **Prove observations with small samples.** For two near-field and two far-field DART stations, inspect headers, UTC handling, cadence changes, gaps, raw/fitted/residual units, and deployment coordinates. Reconcile station IDs and coordinates against the NCEI deployment metadata.
4. **Prove six coastal records.** Start with Saipan, Adak, Hilo/Honolulu, Crescent City, Pago Pago, and one Chilean gauge. Verify a continuous event window, sensor/station history, datum notes, and a primary raw-data URL for each. Reject or replace any gauge whose first deviation cannot be reproduced.
5. **Freeze an arrival-pick protocol before comparison.** Define filtering, baseline window, threshold, sustained-duration rule, gap handling, clock precision, first-deviation uncertainty, and maximum-wave definition. Compare automated picks with NCEI historical arrival fields, whose schema exposes arrival/travel time and predicted travel time but may carry source ambiguity. ([NCEI observation feature schema](https://gis.ngdc.noaa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/web_mercator/hazards/MapServer/2))
6. **Test the discovery, then choose communities.** Compute source distance and modeled/observed first arrival for the candidate pool. Keep only a four-to-six-place set that actually demonstrates a meaningful distance-versus-bathymetry/geography contrast; report residuals and counterexamples, not only the best-looking pair.
7. **Audit warning evidence separately.** Search primary PTWC/NTWC and national/local archives for the exact issued bulletin and, if claimed, local public-alert record. If only an issue timestamp is available, label it center issuance lead. If no primary record exists, omit that metric rather than estimating it from news coverage.
8. **Close terms and provenance.** Save dataset-level metadata, DOI/citation text, credits, retrieval parameters, versions, checksums, and any partner restrictions in the future source manifest. Keep NOAA research-model disclaimers visible in methods and never present retrospective NCTR output as an official operational forecast.

The event should be frozen only after steps 1–6 pass. Steps 7–8 govern which warning-language and redistributable assets may enter the public story.

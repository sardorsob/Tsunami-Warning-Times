## Candidate discovery questions

1. Is the selected event, contour set, and observational chain legible in one
   honest Pacific view?
2. Which station records are sufficiently complete around a source-independent
   arrival window to support comparison?
3. Does geodesic distance meaningfully disagree with published travel-time
   contours for plausible community pairs?
4. After shared review, do observed arrivals preserve, complicate, or reject
   that contrast?
5. If the contrast fails, is uneven observability or the separation of physical
   and warning-system clocks a stronger supported story?

## Promotion rule

2026-10-05 review: shared timing diagnostics strengthen the need to distinguish
an instrument excursion from a validated tsunami arrival. DART 21418 is a
possible future explanatory example, not a promoted scene. The current shared
release explicitly denies arrival-comparison permission, so no community timing
shortlist or modeled-versus-observed scene is approved. Observability remains a
candidate pivot requiring its own story decision and review.

A candidate becomes a storyboard primitive only when its source data, method,
visual artifact, limitations, and decision-ledger entry are reviewed together.
Promotion does not make an exploratory figure an app asset; a separate export
manifest is required.

The final narrative must work without hover, animation, sound, WebGL, or the
submission abstract. It must remain materially distinct from the conference
paper's questions, analysis, figures, and contribution.

# Problem

## Question

How much does a real, depth-dependent tsunami travel-time field differ from a
naive distance-only propagation model, and how closely do selected DART and
tide-gauge arrival observations agree with the modeled field?

## Decision value

The evidence must determine whether a single historical event can support a
clear visual explanation of unequal arrival times around the Pacific and which
community comparisons are defensible.

## Success

- A reader can explain why source distance alone is insufficient.
- Every displayed timing is traceable to a source and a named definition.
- Modeled and observed values remain visually and semantically distinct.
- The central comparison survives a static, reduced-motion presentation.
- The public artifact is functional, accessible, original, and self-explanatory.

## What would mislead

- treating rupture-to-arrival duration as public warning lead time;
- mixing first deviation, maximum wave, forecast, bulletin, and alert times;
- implying false precision from source or detection timestamps;
- showing a decorative wavefront, amplitude, or turbulence not supported by data;
- silently choosing a famous event whose public data cannot support the story;
- hiding missing stations, NoData, datums, CRS issues, or failed comparisons;
- reporting one pooled null percentage that hides absent timestamps,
  structurally inapplicable fields, quarantined rows, or a wholly unavailable
  source asset.
